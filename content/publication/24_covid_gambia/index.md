abstract: |
  Little is known about SARS-CoV-2 infection risk in African countries with high levels of
  infection-driven immunity and low vaccine coverage. We conducted a prospective cohort study of 349
  participants from 52 households in The Gambia between March 2021 and June 2022, with routine weekly
  SARS-CoV-2 RT-PCR and 6-monthly SARS-CoV-2 serology. Attack rates of 45% and 57% were seen during
  Delta and Omicron BA.1 waves respectively. Eighty-four percent of RT-PCR-positive infections were
  asymptomatic. Children under 5-years had a lower incidence of infection than 18-49-year-olds. One
  prior SARS-CoV-2 infection reduced infection risk during the Delta wave only, with immunity from ≥2
  prior infections required to reduce the risk of infection with early Omicron lineage viruses. In an
  African population with high levels of infection-driven immunity and low vaccine coverage, we find
  high attack rates during SARS-CoV-2 waves, with a high proportion of asymptomatic infections and
  young children remaining relatively protected from infection.
